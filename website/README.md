# Website

The static website at the root of `getbattleplan.app` (SPEC §4.3 WEB-1 to WEB-5, ARCH §6.6, D40; ROADMAP tasks 175, 176, 179, 351, 368): the landing page, seven feature pages written to rank for what people search for, the switching guide, support, and the legal pages. The search plan they follow, with the keyword map and what's still to do, is [docs/SEO.md](../docs/SEO.md). Plain HTML and CSS: no framework, no build step, no cookies, no analytics or third-party requests. The landing page's color scheme picker is pure CSS; its one inline script only sets the browser's `theme-color` to match, and the page works the same without it. The Flutter web app is served next to it under `/app/`.

## The look

`site.css` follows the app's Open Field (SPEC §7.4): Lilita One titles on a hard drop in the scheme's `title-drop`, Black Ops One eyebrows in `accent-text`, cards in the scheme's `card` with a 2 px rim and a 4 px lip (the event cards), a 6 px candy edge on feature tiles, coral candy buttons on a lip, and pill chips. Army green is the default and the only scheme on the other pages.

- **Colors:** use the role tokens on `:root` (`--page`, `--band`, `--text`, `--muted`, `--card`, `--rim`, `--card-muted`, `--drop`, `--accent`, `--going`, `--maybe`, and the rest), never a literal color. They're the app's roles from `lib/core/design/schemes.dart`; `:root:has(#s-light:checked)` and the other three re-define them for the picker. Text on `--page` or `--band` uses `--text`, `--muted`, or the page roles (`--accent`, `--going`, `--maybe`); text on `--card` uses `--text` or `--card-muted`. Each pair is 4.5:1 or more in every scheme. Candy faces (`--coral`, `--mint`, …) are the same in every scheme and carry ink (`--ink`) on them.
- **The fan:** the hero's phones are the real screenshots in a CSS fan, as on the Facebook cover, not one big picture: sharp at every pixel density, lighter, and reusing the page's own images. Each phone's place is `--d` (`-2` to `2`, left to right) and `--a` (its distance from the front), which set its offset, tilt, drop, and scale, and every length is a share of the `.stage`'s width, so only `--fan` (the stage's width) changes per screen and the shape never does. Each phone is named for its scheme (`.p-army`, `.p-navy`, …). The picked scheme's phone moves to the front (`--d: 0`) and the others keep the cover's order around it like a ring (Army green, Light blue, Dark, Light, Navy, going right), sliding there over 0.55 s, or at once under `prefers-reduced-motion`; Army green, the default, is the cover itself.
- **The picker:** five radio buttons (`.scheme-input`, visually hidden) come first in the `fieldset`, so plain sibling selectors mark the chips, and focus shows on the chip; Tab reaches the group and the arrow keys move through the schemes. The page colors use `:has()` on `:root`, and the fan's places `:has()` on `.hero-media` (the fan sits before the `fieldset`, outside it); a browser without `:has()` keeps Army green and the cover's fan.
- **Tags:** a chip on each screenshot names its scheme, in that scheme's own colors (`.tag[data-s="navy"]`).
- **Every device:** `viewport-fit=cover` with `env(safe-area-inset-*)` padding on the header, page edges, and footer; tap targets 44 px or more; no horizontal scrolling down to 320 px (the hero and the spotlights clip their overflow); motion off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Editing

- Fonts: use the `--display` (Lilita One), `--stencil` (Black Ops One), `--label` (Fredoka), and `--body` (Nunito) stacks in `site.css`. Don't link Google Fonts or any other CDN: a new weight or font goes in `fonts/` with its license. Black Ops One has capitals only and no middle dot.
- Each page carries its own header and footer. When you change the nav or footer, change it in every HTML file (all fourteen). The footer has two lists, **Features** (the five feature pages) and **Switching** (`/switch` and the two alternatives), over Login, Support, and the legal links; on a page in those lists, its own link has `aria-current="page"`.
- Keep the page `<title>`, `description`, `og:*`, and `twitter:*` tags (with `twitter:image:alt`) in step when you change a page's purpose, and the JSON-LD and `llms.txt` in step with the landing page.
- **FAQs and their JSON-LD:** on every page with an FAQ (the landing page and the feature pages), the `FAQPage` in its JSON-LD repeats each visible question and answer word for word, with links and `.ui` labels as their plain text and `&nbsp;` as a space. Change both in the same edit, and when a feature changes, check every page's FAQ that mentions it (the color scheme answer went stale once).
- Images: give every `<img>` a `width`, `height`, and real `alt` text naming the screen and its scheme (or `alt=""` when decorative). Below the hero, `loading="lazy"`; the hero's five load right away, all but the Army green one (in front to start) with `decoding="async"`, and the Army green phone and the Navy one with `fetchpriority="high"`: browsers count the Navy one as the page's largest image (LCP), since its tilted box is a little bigger than the front phone's. Keep `images/` under about 2 MB.
- Text and UI paths should match the app's English strings (`lib/l10n/app_en.arb`), for example **Settings** → **Delete account**, and the schemes' names.
- On the landing page and the feature pages, say **players** and **guardians**: never kids, children, or parents (the owner's rule, 2026-10-16), even though "parents" is searched more (docs/SEO.md). Accounts are for adults; players who don't use the app are roster entries their guardians answer for.

## The App Store and Google Play

The iPhone and iPad app is on the App Store at `https://apps.apple.com/app/id6823884728` (Apple ID 6823884728, bundle `app.getbattleplan`). The landing page's hero, each feature page's hero, and the support page's Getting the app show Apple's **Download on the App Store** badge linking there (ROADMAP task 353, with the owner's OK to use Apple's artwork, 2026-11-06), and so does the web app's invite page, `/join/<code>`, in a browser that isn't on Android (INV-7). The landing page's FAQ, `llms.txt`, and the JSON-LD link to the listing in plain text; `/ios` in `firebase.json` redirects to it, and the web app's iPhone notification tip opens it (NOTIF-9, `LinkService.iosApp`). Android is "on the way" everywhere until it's on Google Play.

**Android testers needed** (owner's request 2026-11-11): beside Apple's badge in the landing page's hero, a badge of our own (`.tester-badge` in `site.css`: Apple's black, gray rim, and 48 px height, a phone glyph, "Android testers needed" over "Join the beta"; never Google's artwork, which waits for the Play listing) opens `/android-testers`: who can test (18 or older, the US or Canada, an Android phone with Google Play), and how: email support@getbattleplan.app (a prefilled mailto) with your name, your Google Play email, and your phone's model; the owner adds you to the tester list and emails the opt-in link; stay opted in 14 days. No perks, at the owner's word, and no Play testing link or tester details on the page (the repo is public). Take the badge and page down, or point them at the listing, when the app opens on Google Play.

Apple's rules for the badge ([App Store marketing guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/), "App Store badges"), which the pages follow:

- **The artwork as Apple supplies it.** `images/app-store-badge.svg` is the black US-English badge from Apple's marketing tools (`https://toolbox.marketingtools.apple.com/api/v2/badges/download-on-the-app-store/black/en-us`), byte for byte: never recolor, crop, angle, or animate it, and never translate "App Store". The app's copy, `assets/store/app-store-badge.png`, is the same artwork rendered at 3×.
- **The black badge, on every scheme.** Apple prefers it in all marketing, and requires it once another store's badge sits beside it; its gray rim is part of the artwork and keeps it clear on Army green, Dark, and Navy. The white badge is only an alternative, so the page doesn't load it.
- **Size and space.** At least 40 px tall on screen (48 px here, 44 in the app), with a quarter of its height clear all round (12 px), in a subordinate spot under the main message, and one badge per page (the FAQ keeps its plain link).
- **Order.** The App Store badge comes first in a row of badges.
- **Credit line.** Apple asks for a credit line naming Apple, the Apple logo, and every other Apple trademark the page uses. Each page that shows the badge ends its footer with "Apple, the Apple logo, iPhone, and iPad are trademarks of Apple Inc., registered in the U.S. and other countries. App Store is a service mark of Apple Inc." (`.credit`); add any other Apple trademark the page comes to name. The web app's invite page names no other, so its line (`appleCredit`) credits Apple, the Apple logo, and the App Store.

Google Play's badge waits (ROADMAP task 214) in a `<template data-store-badges>` after Apple's, in `index.html` and `support.html`, which the browser never renders or loads. To turn it on:

1. Add the official "Get it on Google Play" artwork to `images/` as `google-play-badge.png`, following Google's badge guidelines, at the same height as Apple's.
2. Check the package (`app.getbattleplan`).
3. Move its `<a>` out of the `<template>` into the `div.store-badges`, after Apple's, in both files, and on the web app's `/join` page for Android browsers.
4. Change the copy that says the Android app is "on the way" (the FAQ and its JSON-LD copy, the support page, `llms.txt`, the README) and set the JSON-LD `operatingSystem` to "iOS, iPadOS, Android, Web".
