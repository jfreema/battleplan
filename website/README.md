# Website

The static website at the root of `getbattleplan.app` (SPEC §4.3 WEB-1 to WEB-5, ARCH §6.6, D40; ROADMAP tasks 175, 176, 179, 351, 368, 464, 477): the landing page, seven feature pages written to rank for what people search for, the switching guide, support, and the legal pages. The search plan they follow, with the keyword map and what's still to do, is [docs/SEO.md](../docs/SEO.md). Plain HTML and CSS: no framework, no build step, no cookies, no analytics or third-party requests. No page runs any script but its JSON-LD. The Flutter web app is served next to it under `/app/`.

## The look

`site.css` follows the app's Boot Camp Mix (SPEC §7.4) in its default scheme, Black, on every page, with the app's Team Shirt lettering (#104) and its candy finish (#98): titles in Lilita One, white letters with an ink outline drawn under the fill (`-webkit-text-stroke` with `paint-order`) and a hard olive shadow; mustard caption tags (`.eyebrow`) in the stencil, tilted; panels (cards, the FAQ, notes, related links) with a 3 px ink edge, square corners, and a hard 5 px olive shadow, rows split by ink lines; candy push buttons (green **Get Started**, mustard **Login** in the header, panel-colored secondary); camouflage with a halftone fade under the landing page's hero and the feature pages' heroes, and an ink line under each; a fine dot grid on the page; the header and footer in the app's tab-bar black. The feature pages' cards have a stripe of color down the left like the schedule's date pennants, and their phones sit on a disc in a bright face with halftone.

- **Lettering** (Team Shirt, #104; owner's go 2026-11-27: "update the site and app to do look 56 Team Shirt with the Lato font.", after "I think there might be too much stencil font everywhere on the landing page and in the app."): three fonts, each with one job. `--display`, Lilita One, is for titles and the names of things: `h1` to `h3`, the related cards' titles, the steps' numbers, and the RSVP picture's question. `--body`, Battleplan Sans (Lato), is everything people read or press, in sentence case: text at 400; labels, the nav, the FAQ's questions, and bold spans at 700; buttons and the links set apart at 900. `--stencil`, Battleplan Stencil, is only for lettering that's stamped on something: the caption tags, the scheme tags under phones, the numbered dog tags, the badge letters, and the starbursts. Before #104 the stencil set every title, button, label, and the nav. The footer's Battle Baseball wordmark is that brand's own stencil and stays.
- **Candy** (the app's finish, `lib/core/design/candy.dart`, #98; here since #104): what you press, and what's stuck on, stands on a lip. Under the face sits a copy of its shape in a darker shade of its own color (the `--*-lip` tokens: the face's HSL lightness × 0.66 and saturation × 0.85, or lightness × 0.46 under a dark face), inside the ink edge; along the face's top runs a 2.5 px highlight line (45% white, 9% on a dark face); and at its top left a short shine (`::after`). The lip and the line are box shadows, which take no room, so each piece leaves a margin under itself. Buttons (`.btn`: a 3 px edge, 17 px corners, a 7 px lip; pressed, the face sinks 3 px into the lip while the ink under it stays, `--sink`; a variant sets `--face`, `--lip`, and `--fg`), the caption tags, the scheme tags and dog tags, the badge tiles, the FAQ's round buttons, the 404 page's links, the RSVP picture, and the starbursts have it. Panels and phones don't: they keep the ink edge and the hard olive shadow, as the app's panels do.
- **Colors:** use the role tokens on `:root` (`--page`, `--band`, `--text`, `--muted`, `--card`, `--rim`, `--card-muted`, `--drop`, `--ink`, `--title`, `--trim`, the bright faces `--red`, `--green`, `--orange`, `--blue`, `--purple`, and the lips `--green-lip`, `--trim-lip`, `--red-lip`, `--orange-lip`, `--purple-lip`, `--card-lip`, `--page-lip`), never a literal color, except the schemes' own colors on the tags and dog tags. They're the app's Black scheme. Text on the page, the band, or a panel uses `--text`, `--muted`, `--card-muted`, `--title`, or `--trim`; lettering on a bright face is ink. Each pair is 4.5:1 or more.
- **Starbursts:** `.burst` (the app's "IN 5 DAYS!" burst), decoration only, `aria-hidden`, sized by `--bs`. The star, its lip, and its shine are one picture, `images/burst.svg`, under the stencil words.
- **Tags:** on the feature pages, a tag on each screenshot names its scheme, in that scheme's own colors (`.tag[data-s="navy"]`). The landing page leaves them off and shows all thirteen as dog tags instead.
- **Every device:** `viewport-fit=cover` with `env(safe-area-inset-*)` padding on the header, page edges, and footer; tap targets 44 px or more; no horizontal scrolling down to 320 px (the hero and the spotlights clip their overflow); motion off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Editing

- Fonts: use the `--display` (Lilita One), `--body` (Battleplan Sans), and `--stencil` (Battleplan Stencil) stacks in `site.css`, each for its job (Lettering, above). Lilita One has one weight: always ask for 400, or the browser fakes a bold (`font-synthesis: none` on the headings guards them). Battleplan Sans has 400, 700, and 900, and the stencil 900. Those are the only weights the site has. Every page preloads `lilita-one.woff2` and `battleplan-sans-regular.woff2`, the title's font and the text's. Don't link Google Fonts or any other CDN: when the app's fonts change, run `tools/make_web_fonts.sh` (it writes the same files every time, so with nothing changed it leaves `fonts/` as it was), and a new font goes in `fonts/` with its license.
- Each page carries its own header and footer. When you change the nav or footer, change it in every HTML file (all fourteen). The footer has two lists, **Features** (the five feature pages) and **Switching** (`/switch` and the two alternatives), over Login, Support, and the legal links, then "© 2026 Battleplan"; on a page in those lists, its own link has `aria-current="page"`.
- **No email addresses for bots to collect** (owner's request 2026-11-26: "hide my email address from the webpage because im getting too much spam"; #103; `support@` and `privacy@` forward to the owner's Gmail). The footers, the landing page's JSON-LD, and `llms.txt` carry none, and nothing links with `mailto:`. Only where a page must say how to reach us (the Support page, which also points to the app's Settings → Contact support, the Privacy Policy, the Terms, Delete your account, and the Android testers' steps) is an address written, as `<span class="addr">support<span class="addr-x" aria-hidden="true">.nospam</span>@getbattleplan.app</span>`: people see, hear, and copy the real address, while the page's source never has it whole, and a scraper that strips the tags gets one that goes nowhere.
- Keep the page `<title>`, `description`, `og:*`, and `twitter:*` tags (with `twitter:image:alt`) in step when you change a page's purpose, and the JSON-LD and `llms.txt` in step with the landing page.
- **FAQs and their JSON-LD:** on every page with an FAQ (the landing page and the feature pages), the `FAQPage` in its JSON-LD repeats each visible question and answer word for word, with links and `.ui` labels as their plain text and `&nbsp;` as a space. Change both in the same edit, and when a feature changes, check every page's FAQ that mentions it (the color scheme answer went stale once).
- Images: give every `<img>` a `width`, `height`, and real `alt` text naming the screen and its scheme (or `alt=""` when decorative). Below the hero, `loading="lazy"`; the hero's three load right away, the two behind with `decoding="async"`, and the front one (the Sky Blue team chat) with `fetchpriority="high"`, as the page's largest image (LCP). Keep `images/` under about 2 MB.
- Text and UI paths should match the app's English strings (`lib/l10n/app_en.arb`), for example **Settings** → **Delete account**, and the schemes' names.
- On the landing page and the feature pages, say **players** and **guardians**: never kids, children, or parents (the owner's rule, 2026-10-16), even though "parents" is searched more (docs/SEO.md). Accounts are for adults; players who don't use the app are roster entries their guardians answer for. The one exception is the owner's line for one vote per player (2026-11-25, shortened at his request 2026-11-26, "its too wordy"): "Polls can count one vote per player, not per parent." on the landing page and the Team chat page.

Android is "on the way" everywhere until it's on Google Play.

**Android testers needed** (owner's request 2026-11-11): beside Apple's badge in the landing page's hero, a badge of our own (`.tester-badge` in `site.css`: Apple's black, gray rim, and 48 px height, a phone glyph, "Android testers needed" over "Join the beta"; never Google's artwork, which waits for the Play listing) opens `/android-testers`: who can test (18 or older, the US or Canada, an Android phone with Google Play), and how: email support@ (written as the protected address, with no button or mailto since #103) with the subject "Android tester", your name, your Google Play email, and your phone's model; the owner adds you to the tester list and emails the opt-in link; stay opted in 14 days. No perks, at the owner's word, and no Play testing link or tester details on the page (the repo is public). Take the badge and page down, or point them at the listing, when the app opens on Google Play.

Apple's rules for the badge ([App Store marketing guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/), "App Store badges"), which the pages follow:

- **The artwork as Apple supplies it.** `images/app-store-badge.svg` is the black US-English badge from Apple's marketing tools (`https://toolbox.marketingtools.apple.com/api/v2/badges/download-on-the-app-store/black/en-us`), byte for byte: never recolor, crop, angle, or animate it, and never translate "App Store". The app's copy, `assets/store/app-store-badge.png`, is the same artwork rendered at 3×.
- **The black badge, on every scheme.** Apple prefers it in all marketing, and requires it once another store's badge sits beside it; its gray rim is part of the artwork and keeps it clear on Army green, Dark, and Navy. The white badge is only an alternative, so the page doesn't load it.
- **Size and space.** At least 40 px tall on screen (48 px here, 44 in the app), with a quarter of its height clear all round (12 px), in a subordinate spot under the main message, and one badge per page (the FAQ keeps its plain link).
- **Order.** The App Store badge comes first in a row of badges.
- **Credit line.** Apple asks for a credit line naming Apple, the Apple logo, and every other Apple trademark the page uses. Each page that shows the badge ends its footer with "Apple, the Apple logo, iPhone, and iPad are trademarks of Apple Inc., registered in the U.S. and other countries. App Store is a service mark of Apple Inc." (`.credit`); add any other Apple trademark the page comes to name. The web app's invite page names no other, so its line (`appleCredit`) credits Apple, the Apple logo, and the App Store.

Google Play's badge waits (ROADMAP task 214) in a `<template data-store-badges>` after Apple's, in `index.html` and `support.html`, which the browser never renders or loads. To turn it on:

1. Add the official "Get it on Google Play" artwork to `images/` as `google-play-badge.png`, following Google's badge guidelines, at the same height as Apple's.
2. Check the package (`app.getbattleplan`).
3. Move its `<a>` out of the `<template>` into the `div.store-badges`, after Apple's, in both files, and on the web app's `/join` page for Android browsers.
4. Change the copy that says the Android app is "on the way" (the FAQ and its JSON-LD copy, the support page, `llms.txt`, the README) and set the JSON-LD `operatingSystem` to "iOS, iPadOS, Android, Web".
