# Website

The static website at the root of `getbattleplan.app` (SPEC §4.3 WEB-1 to WEB-5, ARCH §6.6, D40; ROADMAP tasks 175, 176, 179, 351, 368, 464, 477, 479, 481, 482, 487, 488, 489, 491): the landing page, seven feature pages written to rank for what people search for, the switching guide, support, and the legal pages. The search plan they follow, with the keyword map and what's still to do, is [docs/SEO.md](../docs/SEO.md). Plain HTML and CSS: no framework, no build step, no cookies, no analytics or third-party requests. Each page runs one small script of its own, `scheme.js` (#111), which puts the site in the color scheme the visitor picked on the landing page; without it the site is Black, and everything works. The Flutter web app is served next to it under `/app/`.

## The look

`site.css` follows the app's Boot Camp Mix (SPEC §7.4) on every page, written in the app's default scheme, Black, and shown in Army green unless a visitor picks another (Color schemes, below), with the app's Team Shirt lettering (#104) and its candy finish (#98): titles in Lilita One, white letters with an ink outline drawn under the fill (`-webkit-text-stroke` with `paint-order`) and a hard olive shadow; mustard caption tags (`.eyebrow`) in the stencil, tilted; panels (cards, the FAQ, notes, related links) with a 3 px ink edge, square corners, and a hard 5 px olive shadow, rows split by ink lines; candy push buttons (green **Get Started**, mustard **Login** in the header, panel-colored secondary); camouflage with a halftone fade under the landing page's hero and the feature pages' heroes, and an ink line under each; a fine dot grid on the page; the header and footer in the app's tab-bar black. The feature pages' cards have a stripe of color down the left like the schedule's date pennants, and their phones sit on a disc in a bright face with halftone.

- **Lettering** (Team Shirt, #104; owner's go 2026-11-27: "update the site and app to do look 56 Team Shirt with the Lato font.", after "I think there might be too much stencil font everywhere on the landing page and in the app."): three fonts, each with one job. `--display`, Lilita One, is for titles and the names of things: `h1` to `h3`, the related cards' titles, the steps' numbers, and the RSVP picture's question. `--body`, Battleplan Sans (Lato), is everything people read or press, in sentence case: text at 400; labels, the nav, the FAQ's questions, and bold spans at 700; buttons and the links set apart at 900. `--stencil`, Battleplan Stencil, is only for lettering that's stamped on something: the caption tags, the scheme tags under phones, the numbered dog tags, the badge letters, and the starbursts. Before #104 the stencil set every title, button, label, and the nav. The landing page's footer has Battle Baseball's name as that brand's own picture draws it (#116, owner's request 2026-11-27: "On this part of the website, make the Battle Baseball logo look more like the attached and also have it be a link to https://shop.getbattleplan.app/"): BATTLE in cream Lilita One over BASEBALL in a coral stencil, Black Ops One, each letter in a thick ink outline on a hard ink shadow, as one 4 KB picture, `images/battle-baseball.svg`, a link to the shop at `shop.getbattleplan.app`. The letters are the fonts' outlines, so the page loads neither font for it; `tools/make_battle_baseball.py` draws it from the owner's picture's measurements (within 1% of its pixels). On a dark band the ink outline would be lost, so `site.css` draws the band's hard-shadow color close around the picture (olive in Black); the earlier one was two lines of the site's own stencil, drawn in the page.
- **Candy** (the app's finish, `lib/core/design/candy.dart`, #98; here since #104): what you press, and what's stuck on, stands on a lip. Under the face sits a copy of its shape in a darker shade of its own color (the `--*-lip` tokens: the face's HSL lightness × 0.66 and saturation × 0.85, or lightness × 0.46 under a dark face), inside the ink edge; along the face's top runs a 2.5 px highlight line (45% white, 9% on a dark face); and at its top left a short shine (`::after`). The lip and the line are box shadows, which take no room, so each piece leaves a margin under itself. Buttons (`.btn`: a 3 px edge, 17 px corners, a 7 px lip; pressed, the face sinks 3 px into the lip while the ink under it stays, `--sink`; a variant sets `--face`, `--lip`, and `--fg`), the caption tags, the scheme tags and dog tags, the badge tiles, the FAQ's round buttons, the 404 page's links, the RSVP picture, and the starbursts have it. Panels and phones don't: they keep the ink edge and the hard olive shadow, as the app's panels do.
- **Colors:** use the role tokens on `:root` (`--page`, `--band`, `--text`, `--muted`, `--card`, `--rim`, `--card-muted`, `--drop`, `--ink`, `--title`, `--trim`, the bright faces `--red`, `--green`, `--orange`, `--blue`, `--purple`, and the lips `--green-lip`, `--trim-lip`, `--red-lip`, `--orange-lip`, `--purple-lip`, `--card-lip`, `--page-lip`), never a literal color, except the schemes' own colors on the tags. On `:root` they're the app's Black scheme; `html[data-scheme]` gives them each other scheme's values (Color schemes, below), so a literal color would stay Black's in every scheme. `--burst` is the starburst's picture: coral where the trim is coral, mustard elsewhere (#121). Since #111 there are also `--trim-text` (the trim as words: links' underlines, the footer's headings, a note, and since #121 the keyboard focus ring, which coral alone would leave at 2.9:1 on Army green's panels; darker than `--trim` where a light scheme needs it), `--strong` (a bold span, a question, a card's title: white in Black, the scheme's lettering elsewhere, since white on a white panel has no outline to show it), `--card-text` (lettering on a panel, where the page's is changed around it), `--hero-text` and `--hero-ground` (the heroes' lettering and the camouflage's ground; Sky Blue's heroes are lettered white over its navy-lettered page), `--camo` (the heroes' camouflage), and `--name-filter` (the header's white name turned dark on a light band). An outline, a drop, or ink lettering is `var(--ink)`, never `#000`: some schemes' ink is navy or purple. Text on the page, the band, or a panel uses `--text`, `--muted`, `--card-muted`, `--title`, or `--trim`; lettering on a bright face is ink. Each pair is 4.5:1 or more.
- **Starbursts:** `.burst` (the app's "IN 5 DAYS!" burst), decoration only, `aria-hidden`, sized by `--bs`. The star, its lip, and its shine are one picture under the stencil words, `var(--burst)`: `images/burst-coral.svg` where the trim is coral, `images/burst.svg`, mustard, in the other schemes (#121).
- **Tags:** on the feature pages, a tag on each screenshot names its scheme, in that scheme's own colors (`.tag[data-s="navy"]`). The landing page leaves them off and shows the app in all twelve instead, each named under it.
- **Every device:** `viewport-fit=cover` with `env(safe-area-inset-*)` padding on the header, page edges, and footer; tap targets 44 px or more; no horizontal scrolling down to 320 px (the hero and the spotlights clip their overflow); motion off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Editing

- Fonts: use the `--display` (Lilita One), `--body` (Battleplan Sans), and `--stencil` (Battleplan Stencil) stacks in `site.css`, each for its job (Lettering, above). Lilita One has one weight: always ask for 400, or the browser fakes a bold (`font-synthesis: none` on the headings guards them). Battleplan Sans has 400, 700, and 900, and the stencil 900. Those are the only weights the site has. Every page preloads `lilita-one.woff2` and `battleplan-sans-regular.woff2`, the title's font and the text's. Don't link Google Fonts or any other CDN: when the app's fonts change, run `tools/make_web_fonts.sh` (it writes the same files every time, so with nothing changed it leaves `fonts/` as it was), and a new font goes in `fonts/` with its license.
- Each page carries its own header and footer. When you change the nav or footer, change it in every HTML file (all fourteen). The footer has two lists, **Features** (the five feature pages) and **Switching** (`/switch` and the two alternatives), over Login, Support, and the legal links, then "© 2026 BattlePlan"; on a page in those lists, its own link has `aria-current="page"`.
- **No email addresses for bots to collect** (owner's request 2026-11-26: "hide my email address from the webpage because im getting too much spam"; #103; `support@` and `privacy@` forward to the owner's Gmail). The footers, the landing page's JSON-LD, and `llms.txt` carry none, and nothing links with `mailto:`. Only where a page must say how to reach us (the Support page, which also points to the app's Settings → Contact support, the Privacy Policy, the Terms, Delete your account, and the Android testers' steps) is an address written, as `<span class="addr">support<span class="addr-x" aria-hidden="true">.nospam</span>@getbattleplan.app</span>`: people see, hear, and copy the real address, while the page's source never has it whole, and a scraper that strips the tags gets one that goes nowhere.
- Keep the page `<title>`, `description`, `og:*`, and `twitter:*` tags (with `twitter:image:alt`) in step when you change a page's purpose, and the JSON-LD and `llms.txt` in step with the landing page.
- **FAQs and their JSON-LD:** on every page with an FAQ (the landing page and the feature pages), the `FAQPage` in its JSON-LD repeats each visible question and answer word for word, with links and `.ui` labels as their plain text and `&nbsp;` as a space. Change both in the same edit, and when a feature changes, check every page's FAQ that mentions it (the color scheme answer went stale once).
- Images: give every `<img>` a `width`, `height`, and real `alt` text naming the screen and its scheme (or `alt=""` when decorative). Below the hero, `loading="lazy"`; the hero's three load right away, the two behind with `decoding="async"`, and the front one (the Sky Blue team chat) with `fetchpriority="high"`, as the page's largest image (LCP). Keep `images/` under about 2 MB.
- Text and UI paths should match the app's English strings (`lib/l10n/app_en.arb`), for example **Settings** → **Delete account**, and the schemes' names.
- On the landing page and the feature pages, say **players** and **guardians**: never kids, children, or parents (the owner's rule, 2026-10-16), even though "parents" is searched more (docs/SEO.md). Accounts are for adults; players who don't use the app are roster entries their guardians answer for. The one exception is the owner's line for one vote per player (2026-11-25, shortened at his request 2026-11-26, "its too wordy"): "Polls can count one vote per player, not per parent." on the landing page and the Team chat page.

## Color schemes

The site's default is Army green (#118, owner's request 2026-11-27: "i like the way the theme picker is layed out. let's make the army green one the default theme for now. dont change the order theyre displayed on the site, just have the army green one selected by default. it should clear for a user on the website after a while and not persist for more than a day if possible."; Black, the app's default, until then). Since #111 a visitor can put it in any of the app's twelve schemes by tapping one in the landing page's color schemes band, and it stays that way on every page, for a day at most; then it's Army green again. The app's own default is still Black: this is the website only.

- **How:** `site.css` is written in Black (`:root`), and the generated blocks at its end, one `html[data-scheme="..."]` for each other scheme, give the role tokens that scheme's values. Every page's `<html>` says `data-scheme="army"`, and its `theme-color` is Army green's band, so the default holds with scripts off, for crawlers, and on the first frame. `scheme.js`, in every page's `<head>` so it runs before the page draws, reads a pick from the browser's `localStorage` (`battleplan-scheme`: `<stored value>|<when it was picked, in milliseconds>`) and sets `data-scheme` to it, or takes the attribute off for Black. A pick older than 24 hours is deleted and the page stays as its markup has it; so is one with no time (as #111 stored them), a time in the future, or a scheme that's gone. Tapping the default deletes the pick. A page the browser brings back with Back or Forward looks again.
- **To change the default:** change `data-scheme` on `<html>` and the `theme-color` meta (the scheme's `--band`) in every page, and which button has `aria-pressed="true"` in `index.html`. `scheme.js` takes its default from the page, so it needs no change; for Black, take the attribute off and use `#070707`. It also points the page's `color-scheme` and `theme-color` metas at the scheme, so the browser's own controls and a phone's bar follow.
- **Where the colors come from:** `tools/make_web_schemes.py` reads the app's palettes in `lib/core/design/camp_palette.dart` and writes those blocks and the nine `images/camo-<scheme>.svg`. A scheme whose headers are plain in the app (`CandyTouches.plainHeader`: Royal & Yellow, #117) gets no camouflage file: its heroes are its page color under the page's dots, with no halftone (`--camo: none`, `--halftone: transparent`, and one rule for `.hero` and `.feature-hero`). It also checks `:root`, which is written by hand, against the app's Black palette, and stops if a color there is out of step (as `--red` was when Black's red became coral). The starburst is a picture, so it also writes `images/burst-coral.svg` from `burst.svg` in the app's coral (`SITE_CORAL`, `#ff7f66`), and `--burst` names it in the schemes whose trim is that coral: Black, Army green and Graphite, since the app's Black and Army green took Graphite's coral as their trim and yellow for red (#121, owner 2026-11-27: "the app's color scheme with the yellow-coral swap should change as well for black, army green, and graphite"). The page, band, panels, lettering, trim, bright faces, and camouflage are the palette's own; the lips are worked out as `Candy.lipOf` does; and second lines and the trim as words are stepped in lightness until they read 4.5:1 on what they sit on, as `ColorMath.readable` does. Run it after a palette changes (`python3 tools/make_web_schemes.py`; the same palettes give the same files), and never edit between its markers by hand. When a scheme is added or removed, change its `SCHEMES` list, `scheme.js`'s list, and the band's buttons together.
- **What doesn't change:** the screenshots (they show the app as it is, each in the scheme its tag or caption names), the Playbook band (Army green's camouflage and lettering in every scheme), the App Store badge, and the logo. The starburst is coral in Black, Army green and Graphite, whose trim is coral, and mustard in the other nine, whatever their trim (#121).
- **Privacy:** the pick, with the time it was made, is kept only in the visitor's browser, for a day at most, and never sent anywhere; nothing else is stored, and there are still no cookies, analytics, or third-party requests. With storage blocked the pick lasts for the page; with scripts off the site is Army green.
